# Analysis record and modeling results

## Problem and proposed decision

Our question is which reservations in the dataset are more likely to be canceled. A possible hotel decision is to **prioritize pre-arrival confirmation and review of inventory plans** for bookings with higher validated cancellation risk. The presentation notebook evaluates an illustrative contact threshold, but actual costs, staffing capacity, and the effect of contact remain unmeasured. A high predicted cancellation probability alone does not justify canceling a guest's booking or automatic overbooking.

One row represents one synthetic reservation. The binary target, `booking_status`, uses `1` for canceled and `0` for not canceled. Kaggle identifies `train.csv` as the labeled training file and `test.csv` as the unlabeled prediction file. Our analysis uses only `train.csv` for outcomes. The [Kaggle data page](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/playground-series-s3e7/data) says both files were generated by a deep learning model trained on the [original reservation dataset](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/gauravduttakiit/reservation-cancellation-prediction). Feature distributions are similar but not identical to the original. We have not combined the original data with this project, and we cannot infer that these rates would hold at a real hotel.

## Data audit and cleaning decisions

`train.csv` contains **42,100 rows and 19 original columns**: `id`, 17 candidate predictors, and `booking_status`. The labeled class count is **16,504 canceled (39.2%)** and **25,596 not canceled (60.8%)**. All original columns are numeric in this CSV, although meal plan, room type, and market segment are category codes. There are **no missing cells, duplicate IDs, or exact duplicate labeled rows after excluding `id`**. Removing both `id` and `booking_status` reveals **562 pairs with identical predictor values and opposite outcomes**. This distinction matters when interpreting the Kaggle solution writeups and a random split. Numeric features are nonnegative.

| Finding in `train.csv` | Rows | Decision |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Impossible calendar date from year, month, day | 50 | Keep original fields and flag with `invalid_arrival_date`; do not silently coerce a new date. |
| No adults and no children | 16 | Keep and flag with `zero_guests`; meaning is uncertain. |
| No weekend or weekday nights | 114 | Keep and flag with `zero_nights`; meaning is uncertain. |
| Zero average room price | 641 | Keep and flag with `zero_room_price`; it may reflect a special booking type or a data artifact. |

The main `Graduate_Cancellation_Presentation.ipynb` applies the label-independent transformation directly to raw training data. The archived `archive/00_data_preparation.ipynb` implements the same transformation for train or test and writes generated CSVs to ignored `data/processed/`. Both add `total_guests`, `total_nights`, and four diagnostic flags without dropping or overwriting raw values. No imputation is needed for original missingness in this file. Fixed lead-time and price bands in the archived EDA are descriptive only, not modeling features.

The 17 original candidate predictors are grouped below. Names describe the CSV fields; the numerical codes for categorical fields have no verified business-label mapping in the competition file.

| Field(s) | Working meaning and type |
| --- | --- |
| `no_of_adults`, `no_of_children` | Guest counts; nonnegative integers. |
| `no_of_weekend_nights`, `no_of_week_nights` | Planned stay lengths; nonnegative integers. |
| `type_of_meal_plan`, `room_type_reserved` | Nominal category codes. |
| `required_car_parking_space`, `repeated_guest` | Binary indicators. |
| `lead_time` | Days from booking to planned arrival; nonnegative integer. |
| `arrival_year`, `arrival_month`, `arrival_date` | Components of planned arrival date. |
| `market_segment_type` | Nominal category code. |
| `no_of_previous_cancellations`, `no_of_previous_bookings_not_canceled` | Counts of prior booking outcomes, subject to availability at scoring time. |
| `avg_price_per_room` | Numeric average room price; currency is not specified in the competition CSV. |
| `no_of_special_requests` | Count of recorded requests, subject to availability at scoring time. |

Potential leakage and availability checks:

- Exclude `booking_status` from predictors and `id` because it is an arbitrary row identifier. The first and last ID quartiles have similar outcome rates, but that does not make ID a meaningful feature.
- Arrival year, month, and day are planned dates and could be known when a booking is made. Predictor availability depends on **when** the prediction is made.
- Previous booking counts and special requests may be legitimate pre-arrival features only if their values are available at that decision point. Their exact generation timing is undocumented, which limits claims about deployment.
- Treat `type_of_meal_plan`, `room_type_reserved`, and `market_segment_type` as nominal codes. Do not assign business names to their values without a verified mapping.
- Perform any fitted preprocessing after the train/validation split, ideally in a pipeline. The unlabeled `test.csv` cannot provide validation metrics or observed patterns.

## Descriptive patterns in the labeled file

The archived EDA notebook gives reproducible counts and charts. Each chart places the observed cancellation rate beside its reservation count so sparse groups are visible. The main presentation reproduces the lead-time table with Wilson intervals.

| Lead time before arrival | Reservations | Canceled | Cancellation rate |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 0–7 days | 4,435 | 486 | 11.0% |
| 8–30 days | 4,598 | 1,023 | 22.2% |
| 31–90 days | 11,576 | 2,946 | 25.4% |
| 91–180 days | 14,298 | 7,141 | 49.9% |
| 181–365 days | 7,002 | 4,761 | 68.0% |
| 366+ days | 191 | 147 | 77.0% |

Lead time has the clearest descriptive gradient in the groups investigated. The 366+ day group is small. The same broad pattern appears within the large 2018 subset: 12.9% for 0–7 days (n=3,238), 50.7% for 91–180 days (n=13,039), and 70.2% for 181–365 days (n=6,465). That check does not remove all confounding.

Other patterns worth testing in a multivariable model:

- **Market segment:** code 1 has 50.5% cancellation (n=25,885), code 0 has 22.6% (n=14,306), and code 2 has 11.7% (n=1,384). Codes 3 and 4 have only 83 and 442 rows, respectively. The codes have no verified business labels in this file.
- **Special requests:** zero requests have 47.7% cancellation (n=24,554), one request 30.8% (n=11,885), and two requests 22.9% (n=4,900). Three or more requests are uncommon, so their near-zero rates should not be treated as a reliable policy rule.
- **Average room price:** the band `(90, 120]` has 48.6% cancellation (n=14,819), versus 27.9% for `(60, 90]` (n=13,841). Rates are **not monotonic**: `(150, 200]` is 38.4% (n=3,915). Zero-price rows have a distinct low rate of 4.2% (n=641) and may represent a special case.
- **Arrival year:** 2017 has 18.0% cancellation (n=6,050) and 2018 has 42.8% (n=36,050). This substantial shift warrants a temporal robustness check before relying on random-split metrics.

These are associations within synthetic records. We did not estimate causal effects, assess fairness, or quantify business benefit. The graphs use fixed, interpretable bands and categorical comparisons because the outcome is binary and the question concerns group risk; counts beside rates expose instability in small groups.

## Completed modeling and decision analysis

The main presentation notebook is self-contained and reads `data/raw/train.csv`. It retains all rows, builds the same six derived features, sets `y` to `booking_status`, and removes `id` and the target from `X`. It treats meal plan, room type, and market segment as nominal codes. Imputation, scaling, and one-hot encoding are fitted inside each model pipeline and training fold.

The analysis reserves 60% of labeled rows for fitting, 20% for choosing a decision threshold, and 20% for a random holdout. Three-fold cross-validation on the fitting partition compares a dummy baseline, logistic regression, decision tree, and random forest using average precision (AP) for model selection. The random forest leads this fixed comparison at mean CV AP **0.8304**, versus **0.7913** for the tree, **0.7678** for logistic regression, and **0.3920** for the dummy.

On the 8,420-row random holdout, the selected forest has ROC AUC **0.8882**, AP **0.8352**, and Brier score **0.1327**. Under an *assumed* 5:1 penalty for missed cancellations versus false alerts, validation selects a **0.21** cutoff. On the holdout this flags **61.6%** of reservations, reaches **94.0%** recall and **59.8%** precision, with **2,085** false alerts and **197** missed cancellations. Those penalty units are not dollars or estimated savings. Capacity scenarios in the notebook make the workload tradeoff visible.

The 2017-to-2018 arrival-year stress test has ROC AUC **0.7700** and recall at 0.50 **32.0%**, compared with **0.8882** and **72.2%** on the random holdout. It uses a different sample and prevalence and is not a true booking-time backtest. Earlier full-file exploration and prior examination of the holdout also mean the saved holdout result is not fresh independent confirmation. The 562 opposing-label predictor pairs add another reason to avoid a strong deployment claim from a random split.

The feature sensitivity comparison found no persuasive AP gain from the six derived fields. Removing previous-booking counts and special requests reduced training CV AP from **0.8304** to **0.7606**. Their availability at booking time has not been verified, so the headline score should not be assumed attainable in a booking-time system.

## Remaining decisions

1. Define the scoring time and verify historical feature snapshots, especially previous-booking counts and special requests.
2. Assess genuinely new bookings with a time-aware design, grouping repeated guests or matching predictor records where appropriate.
3. Measure staffing capacity, contact cost, and guest impact; the illustrative 5:1 penalty cannot determine a real policy.
4. Test whether contact changes outcomes in a prospective pilot before claiming operational benefit.
5. Verify any business meanings assigned to category codes or anomalous values before using them in an explanation.
